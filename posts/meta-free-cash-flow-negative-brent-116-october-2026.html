AI Capex & Energy Oct 26, 2026

Meta's Free Cash Flow Went Negative and Brent Hit $116.72. The Fed Decides Into Both.

Record revenue, $34.9 billion of capex, and the first negative free cash flow quarter since 2012. Oil added 6.2% while the 10-year fell under 5%, and the Fed meets Wednesday into both.

Written by Sydney Murphy

The tape has stopped arguing about demand. The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,623.68, down 0.5% and the lowest close of October. The Nasdaq Composite finished at 25,955.34, down 0.7% for a second straight weekly loss, after Thursday's 2.6% drop to 26,008.86, its worst session since June. The Dow closed at 52,215.16, up 0.4% for a third straight weekly gain, which tells you where the money went. The semiconductor index snapped its eight-session streak on Monday, lost 6.7% Thursday, and finished at 10,779.5, down 3.2%.

The damage came from a company that beat. Meta posted record revenue of $64.6 billion, up 26%, and negative free cash flow of $2.1 billion, because $34.9 billion of capex outran $32.8 billion of operating cash flow. That is its first negative quarter since the 2012 IPO, and the stock fell 9.4%. Brent settled at $116.72, up 6.2%, with WTI at $103.27 and energy at a record. The 10-year closed at 4.988%, under 5% for the first time in a month, while gold fell 3.8% to about $4,012. Oil up, yields down, gold down is not generic risk-off. It is one problem being priced.

Scorecard: October 19

Seven rows. Two right, three wrong, two split.

  • S&P 500 holds 7,575 and reclaims 7,765 by Friday. Split. Support held with nearly 50 points to spare. The reclaim missed by 7.83 points: Wednesday's 7,757.17 was the high close.
  • Semiconductor index snaps the eight-session streak and closes higher. Split. It snapped Monday on a 1.6% gain, then lost 3.2% on the week. A session, not a turn.
  • Micron holds $850 on closes. Right, by $6.49. The worst close was $856.49, and Friday added 2.46% to $877.55 while the tape fell.
  • CoreWeave five-year CDS back inside 1,000 basis points. Wrong, in the direction that mattered: a record near 1,194.5, with the 9.75% 2031 notes at a 15.78% yield.
  • 10-year weekly close between 5.00% and 5.25%. Wrong by a basis point, at 4.988%. Treasury's par curve rounded to exactly 5.00%. Right range, wrong side of it.
  • Netflix above $0.82, Meta revenue above $63 billion, Tesla at or above $0.45. Right on all three: $0.87, $64.6 billion, $0.48. Every bar cleared and the stocks fell anyway.
  • Brent weekly close below $112 absent a confirmed Hormuz closure. Wrong by $4.72. No closure was confirmed. Strikes near the strait kept coming and the premium went in anyway.

The Playbook graded worse than the table. Watching CoreWeave's spread rather than Nvidia's chart was the right instrument. Owning Meta into Wednesday was right about the question and wrong about the trade.

Meta Answered the Capex Question by Paying the Bill

Last Monday this column called Meta's capex guide the week's most important number, because it would show whether the hyperscalers fund this buildout from cash flow while the levered neoclouds cannot. The answer arrived as an accounting fact rather than a forecast. There was no 2027 figure. Meta raised the 2026 floor to $135 billion to $145 billion and demonstrated that its own cash flow no longer covers its own buildout. Long-term debt has gone from $28.8 billion at the end of 2024 to $80.9 billion, and a new class of nonvoting Class C stock arrives in January 2027, which is how a balance sheet buys the option to sell equity later.

The Spread Widened Into the Earnings

Meta's five-year credit default swaps hit all-time highs Thursday, as did Nvidia's and Oracle's, but CoreWeave is still the instrument. Its five-year spread hit a record near 1,194.5 basis points, past 11%, which prices better than even odds of default inside five years, and the stock fell about 22% to its lowest since May 2025. Nebius fell roughly 7% on news of a $3.5 billion Citigroup credit line, which is the tell: new funding read as a liability rather than a cushion. Then SK Hynix withdrew the Solidigm IPO, reported near $10 billion. The equity window for AI infrastructure closed in the same week the credit window repriced.

Nvidia sits in the middle by choice, with exposure to the clouds it finances now near $171.5 billion: roughly $7 billion invested and $164.5 billion in guarantees and commitments, including an obligation to buy up to $6.3 billion of CoreWeave capacity if nobody else does. Jensen Huang said Thursday that neocloud demand is off the charts while capital is scarce. Both can be true, and Nvidia still closed at $210.96, its lowest in 20 sessions.

Oil Is the Inflation, and the Long End Does Not Care

The prints were not soft. Claims fell to 194,000, the lowest since July, and the flash composite PMI eased only to 57.6, with selling prices rising at their fastest pace since 2022. Housing was the exception: pending home sales fell 7.3% to an index of 66.0, the lowest since the series began in 2001. Final Michigan sentiment revised up to 48.0, still the second-lowest on record, with year-ahead inflation expectations at 4.7%.

Against that, the 30-year closed at 5.316% for a third straight weekly decline. Gold falling 3.8% in the same week settles the interpretation: this was not a flight to safety, it was money leaving AI credit and buying duration. Energy is the index's best sector at up 45% this year. That leaves Chair Warsh with the supply-side price level the September minutes described, now with Brent $7 higher. Futures put Wednesday's hold near 90% and a December hike near 76%, so the path is not the question. Whether the statement concedes that crude is doing a hike's work is.

My Take

Three weeks ago the problem was breadth, two weeks ago inflation, last week the price of AI credit. This week it is whether the companies that can actually afford the buildout say so convincingly enough to stop the repricing. Meta failed that test by passing the revenue one: a record quarter did not survive $34.9 billion of capex against $32.8 billion of operating cash flow. Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon sit on better math and now have to prove it.

Playbook: treat Microsoft and Alphabet on Wednesday as the real decision of the week rather than the Fed, because a hold is 90% priced and a credible cash-funded capex guide is not; keep Nvidia at BUY with the $268 target at $210.96 while stating plainly that the rating has cost money for three straight weeks, and judge it on the neocloud exposure rather than the demand commentary; hold Micron above $850 on closes, with SanDisk on Thursday the cleaner read on whether contract pricing is intact; own energy as the inflation hedge that is actually working, while respecting that a record sector up 45% prices a lot of Hormuz; buy the 10-year only back above 5.15% rather than chasing 4.988% into a December hike priced at 76%; and fade any AI name whose funding arrives as a new credit facility.
