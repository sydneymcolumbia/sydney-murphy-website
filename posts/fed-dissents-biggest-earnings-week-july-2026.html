A Hawkish Hold on Wednesday, and the Biggest Earnings Week of the Year Around It

The Fed, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Apple, Boeing, PayPal, and Robinhood all report within four days. Here is how I expect each to go and where I am positioned, starting with the one I have the most conviction on.

Written by Sydney Murphy

Alphabet did exactly what I expected on Wednesday: revenue up 24%, Cloud up 82%, and 2026 capex raised to $195 billion to $205 billion. The stock fell 7.7%. Tesla beat on revenue, missed badly on margin, confirmed more than $25 billion of capex, and fell 12%. Intel rose 12% after hours on 85% 18A yields and closed down 8% the next day on foundry losses. Semis are still down roughly 20% for the month. The market has now made its rule completely clear: growth financed by accelerating capex gets sold, no matter how good the growth is.

That rule is the lens for everything this week, and there is a lot of everything: the Fed on Wednesday, Boeing and PayPal tomorrow, Microsoft, Meta, and Robinhood on Wednesday, Amazon and Apple on Thursday, and the first estimate of second-quarter GDP in between.

The Fed: Hold, With Dissents

The June dots had nine of eighteen officials projecting a hike. June CPI then dropped to 3.5% on falling gasoline, which I said at the time bought the Fed time rather than a cut. Since then Brent has gone from $69 to above $100 on the Hormuz attacks before easing back toward $90. That is not a backdrop for a new Chair to sound relaxed.

My expectation is a hold at 3.50% to 3.75% with multiple dissents in favor of a hike, and a statement that explicitly names energy and the Middle East as the reason inflation is not cooperating. That is a hawkish hold. The long end of the curve will not like it, because a Fed that talks tough and does nothing looks like a Fed that is behind. I would not be surprised to see the 10-year through 4.70% by Friday. Long-duration assets, including the stocks reporting this week, have to clear that bar on top of their own numbers.

The Earnings, in Order of My Conviction

  • Microsoft, Wednesday. This is the one. Down close to 20% this year, roughly 30% off its high, and the only hyperscaler that has not raised equity. If Azure growth comes in at or above 40% and capex guidance is held rather than raised, it is the one megacap that satisfies the market's rule, and the setup for a large move higher is as good as I have seen in this name since I first wrote it up last September. I added on July 6 and I am holding through the print.
  • Amazon, Thursday. AWS has been accelerating and the retail margin story is intact. The risk is a capex figure north of $200 billion. I am long, sized smaller than Microsoft.
  • Meta, Wednesday. The problem child. It ran 15% on the cloud-capacity report in early July, which means expectations are high going into a print where costs, including a large headcount reduction, will be up a lot. I would not be long into this one and I am not.
  • Apple, Thursday. Strong iPhone quarter is likely. The thing I am watching is guidance commentary on memory costs. Apple is the largest buyer of DRAM and NAND on earth, and a memory shortage that is great for Micron is a margin problem for Apple. If Tim Cook says it out loud, that is your confirmation of the memory cycle from the demand side.
  • Boeing, Tuesday. I have been long since May. Deliveries have been rising every month, the backlog is a record, and the Hormuz reopening, however fragile, helps Gulf deliveries. A positive free cash flow number would be the turning point I have been waiting for.
  • PayPal, Tuesday. There is a take-private bid on the table at $60.50 and the board wants more. The quarter matters less than the deal. I hold a small position that is now a merger-arbitrage bet with a decent fundamental floor: the stock was among the most oversold in the index before the bid.
  • Robinhood, Wednesday. I wrote in December that the correction was a buying opportunity. Crypto revenue will be down hard given Bitcoin's first half. The offset is prediction markets and equity volumes, and I think the offset is bigger than the market expects. Long, holding.

My Take

The market has decided that accelerating capex is a sin and growth without it is a virtue. That rule punished Alphabet and Tesla last week and will sort this week's reports the same way. A hawkish Fed hold on Wednesday raises the bar for everyone.

Positioning: highest conviction long Microsoft into Wednesday; long Amazon, Boeing, PayPal, and Robinhood; no position in Meta or Apple into their prints.
